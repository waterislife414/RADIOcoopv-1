## Statute check

### Georgia
1. O.C.G.A. 34-8-35(f) (unemployment). Verified (2025 Code, Justia).
   URL: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-34/chapter-8/article-2/section-34-8-35/
   Paraphrase: services performed for wages are presumed to be employment. The employer must rebut the presumption. One route is showing the worker is free of control or direction in the work, under the contract and in fact, and is in an independently established business. The text lists factors such as working for others, setting hours, refusing assignments, and little direction. The other route is an IRS SS-8 determination of non-employee status. Short quote: "customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession, or business."

   URL: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-34/chapter-9/article-1/section-34-9-1/

## (a) Business description
Radio Co-op Taxi Service operates in Columbus, GA and Phenix City, AL. The owner is Malcolm. Drivers are treated as 1099 contractors. They rent a vehicle and a radio from Malcolm for a daily fee called the "lick" (about $75 per day). A dispatcher takes phone calls and assigns the next-up driver in each zone. A rider and dispatch app is being built to add online booking. Most drivers have signed a short paper covering the lick and vehicle responsibility.

## (b) Facts supporting contractor status
- Drivers clock in and out when they choose.
- Drivers can decline calls.
- Drivers can work other jobs.
- Drivers can bring their own regulars.
- Some drivers work without using the radio.
- Drivers pay the daily fee for the vehicle and radio, handle maintenance, and pay 100% of damage they cause. Malcolm covers normal wear and tear.

## (c) Facts weighing toward employee status
- The dispatcher assigns calls by next-up order within a zone.
- There are check-in and conduct standards.
- The dispatcher can remove a driver from the clock for interfering with calls, breaking check-in or conduct rules, or not paying the lick.
- Drivers can be removed for rudeness, missed inspections, or unpaid lick.
- Malcolm can end a lease after a wreck, a jail arrest, or serious conduct, and decides on re-admission.
- Drivers submit an odometer reading and a full vehicle inspection every week.
- Points and free lick days are tied to ratings and inspection compliance.
- The daily fee is fixed.

## (d) Current practice (neutral)
- Drivers pay the lick daily; the amount is about $75.
- Most sign a short paper on the lick and vehicle responsibility. It is unknown whether a formal vehicle lease exists elsewhere.
- Weekly odometer and inspection submissions are treated as a vehicle-lease duty.
- The first weeks of the points system are lenient (planned temporary grace).
- Planned: points and free lick days tied to ratings and ride counts.
- Planned (owner's wish): pre-checked marketing-text consent boxes at sign-up.

2. Does the 100% damage charge need a cap, an itemized estimate, or a dispute process?
3. Does a formal vehicle lease exist? Is any lessor license needed?

8. What is Malcolm's commercial auto coverage (radio calls, app rides, on-call time, passengers, named insureds)?
